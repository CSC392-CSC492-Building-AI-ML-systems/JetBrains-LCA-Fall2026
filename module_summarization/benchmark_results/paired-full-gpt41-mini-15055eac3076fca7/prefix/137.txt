Image Pixel-wise fit for Atmospheric Cherenkov Telescopes (ImPACT)
====================================================================

Overview
--------

The ImPACT (Image Pixel-wise fit for Atmospheric Cherenkov Telescopes) reconstruction method is a Monte Carlo template-based image fitting technique designed for the analysis of atmospheric Cherenkov telescope data. It performs a maximum likelihood fit of the recorded shower images against a library of simulated image templates to reconstruct key shower parameters such as the shower axis, energy, and height of the shower maximum.

This approach was introduced by Parsons & Hinton (2014) and provides a powerful alternative to traditional Hillas parameter-based reconstruction methods by exploiting detailed pixel-wise information in the camera images.

Key Features
------------

- **Template-based fitting:** Uses precomputed image templates generated from Monte Carlo simulations for different telescope types and shower parameters.
- **Maximum likelihood estimation:** Fits the observed pixel amplitudes to the predicted templates by maximizing the likelihood, accounting for noise and pedestal distributions.
- **Multi-parameter reconstruction:** Simultaneously reconstructs shower direction, energy, and depth of shower maximum (Xmax).
- **Support for multiple telescope types:** Includes templates for various camera types such as LSTCam, NectarCam, FlashCam, and CHEC.
- **Incorporation of timing information:** Optionally uses time gradient templates to improve reconstruction accuracy.
- **Atmospheric profile integration:** Converts height above ground to atmospheric depth using site-specific atmospheric profiles.

Methodology
-----------

The ImPACT method compares the observed camera images to a library of simulated templates that represent expected Cherenkov light distributions for showers with varying parameters. The fitting procedure involves:

1. **Template interpolation:** For each telescope type, image templates are interpolated over shower parameters such as energy, impact distance, and shower maximum depth.
2. **Likelihood calculation:** The likelihood of the observed pixel signals given the template prediction is computed, incorporating pedestal noise and single photo-electron resolution.
3. **Parameter optimization:** The shower parameters are optimized by maximizing the likelihood using numerical minimizers such as Minuit or least squares methods.
4. **Use of priors:** Optional priors on energy and shower maximum depth can be applied to guide the fit and improve stability.
5. **Coordinate transformations:** Shower geometry is handled using astropy coordinate frames, including NominalFrame and TiltedGroundFrame, to accurately model the shower axis and telescope positions.

Units and Conventions
---------------------

To optimize computational performance, the ImPACT implementation uses fixed units internally:

- Angular measurements are in **radians**.
- Distances are in **metres**.
- Energies are in **TeV**.

This approach avoids the overhead of astropy unit conversions during intensive numerical calculations.

Atmospheric Profiles
--------------------

The conversion between height above ground and atmospheric depth (g/cm²) is essential for interpreting the shower maximum depth. ImPACT uses site-specific atmospheric profiles (e.g., Paranal) obtained from CORSIKA simulations to perform this conversion accurately.

Priors
------

ImPACT supports the use of priors to constrain the fit parameters:

- **Energy prior:** A power-law prior on the shower energy can be applied to reflect expected spectral indices.
- **Xmax prior:** A Gaussian prior on the depth of shower maximum based on an empirical elongation rate relation.

These priors help regularize the fit and improve reconstruction robustness.

Usage Notes
-----------

- The method is computationally intensive due to the pixel-wise likelihood calculations and template interpolations.
- It is recommended to initialize templates for each telescope type before processing events.
- Timing information can be optionally included to enhance reconstruction accuracy.
- The method currently uses hard-coded pedestal widths and single photo-electron resolution values per camera type, which may need calibration updates.

Summary
-------

ImPACT provides a sophisticated and accurate reconstruction technique for atmospheric Cherenkov telescope data by leveraging detailed Monte Carlo templates and maximum likelihood fitting. It enables precise estimation of shower parameters critical for gamma-ray astronomy and cosmic ray studies.
