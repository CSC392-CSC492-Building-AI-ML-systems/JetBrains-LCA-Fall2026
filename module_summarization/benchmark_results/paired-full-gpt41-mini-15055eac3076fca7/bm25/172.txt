SANS to SESANS Conversion
=========================

This document describes the conversion process from Small-Angle Neutron Scattering (SANS) data to Spin-Echo Small-Angle Neutron Scattering (SESANS) correlation functions and polarization, using a Hankel transformation.

Overview
--------

The conversion involves transforming the scattering cross section measured in a SANS experiment, typically represented as the intensity \( I(q) \) or differential scattering cross section \( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \) in absolute units (cm\(^{-1}\)), into the SESANS correlation function \( G(z) \). This correlation function is then used to calculate the SESANS polarization, which is the experimentally measured quantity in SESANS.

Units
-----

- Length units in the calculations are primarily in metres, except where otherwise specified.
- Conventional units such as nanometres (nm) are used for spin echo length \( z \).
- The SANS intensity \( I(q) \) is assumed to be in absolute units (cm\(^{-1}\)).

Mathematical Background
-----------------------

The SESANS correlation function \( G(z) \) is obtained from the SANS intensity \( I(q) \) by performing a Hankel transform of order zero:

\[
G(z) = \int_0^\infty q \, I(q) \, J_0(q z) \, dq
\]

where

- \( q \) is the scattering vector magnitude,
- \( z \) is the spin echo length,
- \( J_0 \) is the zeroth-order Bessel function of the first kind.

This integral transforms the reciprocal space information from SANS into real-space correlations accessible by SESANS.

Implementation Details
----------------------

- The transformation is implemented in the `SesansTransform` class.
- The class takes as input the SANS intensity \( I(q) \) sampled at a set of \( q \) values.
- The Hankel transform is computed numerically using the Bessel function \( J_0 \) from the `scipy.special` module.
- The resulting SESANS correlation function \( G(z) \) can then be converted into the SESANS polarization \( P(z) \), which relates to the measured spin echo polarization in SESANS experiments.

Precision and Numerical Considerations
--------------------------------------

- The numerical integration and Bessel function evaluations are carefully handled to maintain precision.
- For small arguments, series expansions of spherical Bessel functions are used to avoid loss of precision.
- The method is suitable for typical SANS and SESANS ranges, where the scattering objects are on the order of 100 nm or larger, and the \( q \)-range is limited accordingly.
- Relative errors in the numerical methods are controlled to ensure accuracy in the SESANS correlation function.

Usage Notes
-----------

- The input SANS data should be in absolute units and properly normalized.
- The transformation assumes isotropic scattering; oriented or anisotropic data require additional considerations.
- The SESANS correlation function and polarization provide complementary real-space information about the sample structure, accessible through SESANS experiments.
