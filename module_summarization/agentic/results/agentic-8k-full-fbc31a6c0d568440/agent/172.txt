SANS to SESANS Conversion
=========================

This document describes the conversion process from Small-Angle Neutron Scattering (SANS) data to Spin-Echo Small-Angle Neutron Scattering (SESANS) correlation functions and polarization, using a Hankel transform approach.

Overview
--------

The conversion involves transforming the scattering cross section from SANS, typically represented as the intensity \( I(q) \) or differential scattering cross section \( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \) in absolute units (cm\(^{-1}\)), into the SESANS correlation function \( G \). This is achieved through a Hankel transformation. Subsequently, the SESANS correlation function is converted into the polarization \( P \) measured in SESANS experiments.

Units
-----

- Length units in the calculations are primarily in meters, except where otherwise specified.
- Spin-echo lengths are conventionally expressed in nanometers (nm).
- Wavevector \( q \) and related quantities are handled in reciprocal length units consistent with the input data.

Key Concepts
------------

- **SANS Intensity \( I(q) \)**: The scattering intensity as a function of the scattering vector magnitude \( q \).
- **SESANS Correlation Function \( G \)**: The real-space correlation function obtained by transforming \( I(q) \).
- **Polarization \( P \)**: The SESANS measurable quantity related to \( G \) by \( P = G - G_0 \), where \( G_0 \) is a baseline correlation.

SesansTransform Class
---------------------

The `SesansTransform` class encapsulates the transformation process:

### Initialization

```python
SesansTransform(z, SElength, lam, zaccept, Rmax, log_spacing=1.0003)
```

- `z` : array_like  
  The input \( q \)-values corresponding to the SANS data.
- `SElength` : array_like  
  The set of spin-echo lengths at which the SESANS data is measured.
- `lam` : float  
  The neutron wavelength used in the experiment.
- `zaccept` : float  
  The maximum acceptance of the scattering vector in the spin-echo encoding dimension.
- `Rmax` : float  
  The maximum size sensitivity for the transform; larger values increase computation time.
- `log_spacing` : float, optional  
  The logarithmic spacing factor for \( q \)-values used in the Hankel transform.

### Methods

- `apply(Iq)`  
  Applies the SESANS transform to the input intensity \( I(q) \) and returns the polarization \( P \).

### Internal Computation

- The Hankel transform matrices `_H` and `_H0` are computed during initialization based on the input parameters.
- The transform uses Bessel functions of the first kind (order zero) to perform the integral transform from reciprocal space to real space.
- The acceptance mask ensures that contributions beyond the maximum accepted scattering vector \( zaccept \) are zeroed out.

Mathematical Details
--------------------

The SESANS correlation function \( G \) is computed as:

\[
G = \int_0^\infty q \, I(q) \, J_0(q z) \, dq
\]

where \( J_0 \) is the zeroth-order Bessel function of the first kind, and \( z \) is the spin-echo length.

The polarization \( P \) is then:

\[
P = G - G_0
\]

where \( G_0 \) corresponds to the baseline correlation function.

Usage Notes
-----------

- The transform assumes that the input SANS data \( I(q) \) is provided on a suitable \( q \)-grid.
- The choice of \( Rmax \) affects the resolution and computational cost.
- The logarithmic spacing parameter controls the density of \( q \)-points used internally for the Hankel transform.
- The method accounts for instrumental acceptance limits via the `zaccept` parameter.
